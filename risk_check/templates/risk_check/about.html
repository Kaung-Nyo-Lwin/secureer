01 / THE QUESTION

Where could your skills take you?

Automation changes the tasks people do, but a single score cannot describe a whole career. Secureer pairs an experimental exposure assessment with related roles and skills, giving people a way to explore possible next steps.

The original class project combined MyJob’s Myanmar job-listing data with occupation-level computerisation research from Frey and Osborne and nine O*NET bottleneck descriptions. This portfolio edition makes that work easier to try and inspect.

02 / HOW IT WORKS

Two modes. Clear boundaries.

DEFAULT · NO MODEL DOWNLOAD

Offline reference demo

Your title is compared with the 702 historical occupations using normalized keyword overlap. A sufficiently close match supplies its historical reference index; an unfamiliar title receives no score. Your skills rank related roles using inverse-frequency weighted keyword similarity.

This mode demonstrates the product flow. It does not run the original ML model, and skills do not change its exposure score.

OPTIONAL · ORIGINAL ML PIPELINE

Semantic career exploration

The all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence transformer encodes titles and skills. K-means assigns a title to one of 20 occupation clusters; a nearest-neighbor model finds five related roles by skill similarity. Both algorithms are fitted to the included datasets when the engine first loads.

The exposure index averages the cluster’s historical occupation score and the cosine distance between your skills and nine O*NET bottleneck descriptions. The distance is bounded to 0–1, and the final index is displayed on a 0–100 scale.

Both modes identify shared skills and suggest additional skills found in the matched roles. Recommendations are ranked by skill similarity, not by exposure.

04 / INTERPRETATION

A conversation starter, with limits.

  • An index, not an individual forecast. Neither mode estimates your personal chance of losing a job or a date when a role will be automated.
  • Historical context matters. The underlying occupation research predates recent generative AI developments and describes a different labor market. Transferring those scores to Myanmar is an experimental assumption.
  • Similarity is not suitability. Matching words or embeddings cannot establish your qualifications, interests, salary expectations, or the availability of a role.
  • No claimed predictive accuracy. This repository does not establish real-world job-loss outcomes or calibrated individual risk. Lower and higher bands are interface conventions: ≤40, >40–70, and >70.

05 / YOUR DATA

Only the details the demo needs.

Your first name is optional; a nickname works. Submitting an assessment stores the name you provide, role, skills, and report in this app’s database. A session cookie limits access to your reports to the browser session that created them. The site operator can access stored records.

Reports are kept on the server until deleted; closing the browser does not delete them. Use Delete this report on the results page before ending your session to remove the profile and report. The browser session remembers up to 20 recent reports; older records remain stored until the site operator removes them. The public example report does not save a profile.

There are no analytics scripts or third-party fonts. Inference runs on the application server. ML mode may download model files on its first use; profile text is not sent to a hosted AI service.
